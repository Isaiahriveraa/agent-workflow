# Approved examples and candidate references

Use references to explain a specific decision, not to copy an entire product. **No examples are user-approved yet.** Candidate sources below are starting points for review, not claims about the user's taste or verified live behavior.

## Record a useful example

Keep project-specific choices in its existing design brief or design-system notes. Keep cross-project preferences here only after explicit user approval. Do not create a parallel approval queue.

For each example, record:

- **Source:** exact URL, relevant screen/interaction, and capture date. Use an authorized screenshot for layout or a short recording for motion; text alone cannot establish timing.
- **What we like:** the specific hierarchy, wording, feedback, or transition, with the user's words where available.
- **What to borrow:** one transferable principle and the problem it solves.
- **What not to copy:** irrelevant colors, branding, content, physics, delays, or hidden assumptions.
- **Where it fits:** audience, task, platform, and situations where it would be distracting or confusing.
- **Evidence and approval:** what was actually inspected; proposed, approved, or rejected; who decided and when. Model suggestions remain proposed.

## Five proposed starting points

| Candidate source | Suggested lesson to examine | Do not assume or copy |
|---|---|---|
| [Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/motion/overview/) | Restrained response for frequent task actions versus an expressive one-off moment | IBM's brand, exact curves, and timings as universal defaults |
| [NN/g motion examples](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-purpose-ux/) | A panel's origin and destination can preserve orientation | That every navigation change needs movement |
| [Rauno's Craft: Toolbar Morph](https://rauno.me/craft) | Examine whether continuity helps people track the same control through a change | Experimental morphing on every button or unverified keyboard behavior |
| [Emil Kowalski / animations.dev](https://animations.dev/) | Public drawer and feedback examples as candidates for responsive, interruptible motion | Paid course material, implementation details, or behavior not actually inspected |
| [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) | Compare a relevant shipped student-task flow, including wording and recovery, not just its first screen | That popularity proves usability, or that an adult productivity flow suits all learners |

Status for all five: **proposed; public page text consulted 2026-10-15; no recordings captured or live interactions tested.** Mobbin may require a paid plan for particular flows. Do not bypass access controls, copy paid assets, or invent screenshot evidence.

When a visual direction remains unproven, follow the existing prototype gate. A reference's approval approves the named lesson, not every future implementation that resembles it. If preferences conflict, use the current project's brief and ask only about the consequential unresolved choice.

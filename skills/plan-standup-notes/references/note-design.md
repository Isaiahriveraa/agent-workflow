# Note-design evidence

Use this guidance to keep plan notes useful for learning rather than merely shorter.

- **Limit competing details.** Working memory is limited; chunking and eliminating irrelevant detail reduce avoidable cognitive load, especially for newer learners. [Designing Instruction That Supports Cognitive Learning Processes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC164417/)
- **Show process and reason.** A compact worked example that pairs each important step with why it happens helps a learner form a reusable problem-solving schema. Do not turn this into a complete reproduction of the plan. [Ten quick tips for creating an effective lesson](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6459472/)
- **Require recall.** A short explain-back and unanswered self-check questions make the learner retrieve and reconstruct the idea; retrieval practice improves long-term retention and transfer over repeated study alone. [Karpicke & Blunt, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/); [Roediger & Butler, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20951630/)
- **Do not oversell the iPad.** A 2022 meta-analysis found no performance difference between longhand and digital note taking under controlled, distraction-free conditions. The goal is active, concise stylus notes—not a claim that the device itself guarantees learning. [Urry et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2021.102025)

The evidence supports the skill’s format, not a fixed word count. Adjust density to the complexity and the learner’s familiarity, while preserving one clear system story, material tradeoffs, and recall practice.
